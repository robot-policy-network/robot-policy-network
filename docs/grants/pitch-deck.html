The design

Evidence and authority are different keys

evaluator signs EvaluationAttestation → evidence · CANNOT move funds payer signs ReleaseAuthorization → authority · insufficient alone release() executes only when BOTH validate

The evaluator key is a hot key on our server. The design assumes it will be compromised and makes that specific failure survivable: a compromised evaluator can forge a result on an open job, but still cannot pay itself — the payer's separate signature is required.

What this does not claim: that the score is trustless. See the next two pages.

Mechanism — evaluation

Score by execution, and make faking it expensive

score = 100 × (seedsClean / n) × (0.38·align + 0.32·pick + 0.20·precision + 0.10·eff)

Agents submit a structured action plan. A pure-function simulator executes it against a world state across several perturbation seeds and returns the traced path, the targets actually serviced, and any constraint violations.

Same input → same output, always. Seeded PRNG, no clock, no global randomness. The weights are a tuned heuristic chosen by the founder, not derived from first principles — stated plainly because a reviewer will ask why 0.38 and not 0.40.

Mechanism — each term closes an attack

Nothing in the formula is decorative

AttackClosed by
Submit a plan that does not runseedsClean/n is multiplicative — a hard violation on any seed cuts the whole score; on all seeds it is zero
Draw a straight line between targetsalign = coverage + progression. Coverage alone lets a straight line score 0.95
Service the right targets in the wrong orderpick = LCS on the sequence, not membership
Reuse a winning plan on the next missionprecision uses per-target constants (offset=stem_mm, force=2·stem_mm·(2−ripe)) — different targets, different numbers
Copy a plan inside the same windowthe race closes on a strictly better score — an exact copy cannot displace its original
Drive extra laps for efficiency markseff is an asymmetric ratio capped at 1 — undershooting is free, overshooting costs
Reverse-engineer the rubricfeedback names only public-state terms, never reference coordinates
Arbitrage integer roundingranking uses 2-decimal scores; a generic plan lands 95–97, which would otherwise be a mass tie

Known gap, stated: an exact copy is neutralised, a near-copy plus a marginal tweak is not. Plans are not published per-window, which limits but does not eliminate it.

Mechanism — the competition

Why the race closes on best score, not first past the post

Because first-past-the-post made it a latency contest.

An agent

closes the loop in ~90 seconds.

A person on a phone

pasting a brief into a chat model needs 2–3 minutes.

So a human could never win one, however good the plan. Deciding at the close makes submission time worth nothing and plan quality worth everything — the only version where the two compete in one lane.

The moat

The moat is the combination — and the clock

  • A deterministic, execution-anchored evaluator — most "scoring" projects use a human panel or a non-deterministic LLM call, which cannot anchor an on-chain artifact
  • An on-chain evidence/authority split — deployed and executed; a protocol-level property, not a UI choice
  • Arc-native settlement — low-cost, fast-finality, native USDC

Each alone is copyable; the three together, already frozen and running, are harder. But the honest moat is not the code — it is open-source. It is the time-stamped record of verified outcomes and the operator community that a late entrant cannot backfill.

Counterweight: on-chain, that record is currently ~60 NFT mints and one founder-funded settlement. The 65,138 rounds live in a published but operator-controlled dataset. The moat is early, not built.

Why Arc

Why Arc, and not Base where AI2Human already runs

  • Product reason: the settlement leg needs a stablecoin that is the gas asset and a fast-finality chain, so a machine payment is one asset and one hop. Arc gives native USDC with an ERC-20 interface over the same balance.
  • Integration reason: we hit Arc's USDC compliance precompile (0x1800…0001::isBlocklisted) inside transferFrom and documented what it breaks in local simulation vs. real execution — field notes other Arc builders can use.
  • Not a reason: grant fit. AI2Human is on Base because its users are there. This protocol is on Arc because that is where machine settlement is cheapest.
